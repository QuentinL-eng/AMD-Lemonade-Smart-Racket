# Small player trial

Status: prepared protocol; no participants or results collected. Recruit a few recreational players, describe how each was recruited, and report the actual sample size. This is a usability trial, not evidence of improved tennis performance.

## Ask for permission

"I'm testing a smart racket that explains recorded shot data using a local model. Participation is optional. You can stop at any point. I'd like to note where the app helps or confuses you. May I record your screen and comments for private product review? Separately, may I publish an agreed clip or quote in the challenge demo?"

Record permission for private review and publication separately. Participants may test without appearing in any video. Use participant codes rather than names in the shared results. Do not collect injury histories or other unnecessary personal information.

## Tasks

1. Explore a labeled replay, then identify which values are measured, predicted, or illustrative. Record misunderstandings without correcting them until the task ends.
2. Ask for the shot count and check the displayed value against the session summary. Ask which stroke needs attention and open the supporting data.
3. Ask a what-if question, inspect the proposed change and estimate, then explain in your own words what the app can and cannot predict.
4. If real racket data is available, repeat with that participant's session. Keep replay observations and hardware observations separate.

For each task record completion, time, help needed, wrong answers, and whether the participant found the cited evidence. Ask: "What would you do next?", "What felt confusing?", and "What would make you trust this answer?" Ask for an optional clarity rating from 1 to 5 after each task.

## Results sheet

Use [player-trial.csv](player-trial.csv). Leave unknown fields blank. Keep original answers, including errors. Note app/model versions and the session source. Review the exact wording and scope of each public quote with its speaker before publishing.

Report counts with denominators and short observations. Avoid claims that a convenience sample represents all players, or that a physics estimate proves an actual ball outcome. Show a failure and the resulting product change alongside successful tasks.
